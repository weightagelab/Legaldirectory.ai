Frequently Asked Questions — AP CDMA PTIN

PTIN (Property Tax Identification Number) is the unique reference number assigned by Andhra Pradesh CDMA to every property assessed under an Urban Local Body. It is the primary identifier in the municipal system linking a specific property to its ownership assessment, tax payment history, arrear status, and ULB compliance record.
A PTIN is the municipal tax system's unique identifier for an assessed property. It links the building and plot to its tax assessment, payment history, and owner record in the CDMA system. A survey number is a revenue department identifier for land parcels; a door number is an address identifier. Only the PTIN connects a property to its full municipal compliance history.
A property without a PTIN has not been assessed for property tax by the Urban Local Body, meaning it does not exist in ULB municipal records as an assessed property. This can happen if the property is newly constructed and assessment is pending, if the area was recently absorbed into ULB jurisdiction, or if the property was built without proper authorisation and was never formally assessed.
Yes — this is one of the most common and consequential discrepancies in Andhra Pradesh urban property records. When a property is sold, the Registration Department records the new owner in the sale deed. But the ULB PTIN record continues to show the previous owner until a formal mutation is completed with the ULB updating the assessment to reflect new ownership.
PTIN verification before purchase reveals four things no other document shows: whether the property is officially assessed and exists in ULB records; whether the assessed owner matches the seller; whether outstanding tax arrears exist that will transfer to you as the new buyer; and what the ULB has recorded as the property's built-up area and classification.
Yes — property tax arrears in Andhra Pradesh are a charge on the property, not just on the individual owner. When a property is sold, outstanding arrears transfer to the new owner irrespective of whether the buyer was aware of them. PTIN verification before purchase is the only reliable way to detect and quantify such arrears before they become your liability.
Annual Rental Value (ARV) is the notional annual rent the ULB estimates the property could command in the open market, used as the basis for calculating property tax. The ARV is determined by the ULB based on property location, built-up area, construction type, age, and occupancy. Higher ARV means higher property tax liability.
Errors in PTIN records — such as wrong owner name, incorrect built-up area, misclassified property type, or outdated ARV — must be corrected through a formal application to the ULB assessment section. Supporting documents like sale deed, building plan, and physical measurement reports are required to substantiate correction requests.
PTIN applies exclusively to properties within Urban Local Body jurisdictions in Andhra Pradesh — including municipal corporations, municipalities, and Nagar Panchayats. Agricultural land outside ULB limits is governed by Revenue Department records such as Pahani, Adangal, and ROR 1B, and does not carry a PTIN.
PTIN is issued and managed by each Urban Local Body separately. While CDMA-AP standardises the system across all ULBs, the PTIN for a property in Vijayawada Municipal Corporation is distinct from one in Visakhapatnam Municipal Corporation. The format and length may vary slightly by ULB, but the PTIN remains the primary property identifier within each ULB's jurisdiction.
